How to test perfumes without overwhelming your nose

Updated October 17, 2026 · By Althamir GT

After smelling several perfumes in a row, they all start to seem the same. It's called olfactory fatigue and a few simple habits prevent it.

Why it happens

Your nose adapts to the smells it perceives over time and stops noticing them clearly. It's a normal mechanism: it's why you sometimes stop smelling your own perfume even though other people do.

How to test better

  1. Try a maximum of three or four scents per session.
  2. Apply each one in a different spot: forearms or wrists, and a paper strip only to rule things out quickly.
  3. Wait at least 10 minutes before forming an opinion and, preferably, smell again after one or two hours.
  4. Between one scent and the next, rest a few minutes and breathe fresh air.

What to do if you can no longer tell scents apart

  • Take a break of several minutes in a ventilated place.
  • Smell your own clothes or your bare skin to get back a neutral reference.
  • Some people use coffee beans between tests, but their effect isn't proven; resting is the most reliable approach.

Write down what you try

Note the name, the notes you perceive and your impression at the one-hour and three-hour marks. Over time you'll discover which families you like and you'll be able to choose with more confidence. For a starting point, take the perfume quiz.

Frequently asked questions

Can I test several perfumes on the same area of skin?

It's better to keep them apart: one perfume per area, so they don't blend and you can tell them apart.

Is it better to test on a paper strip or on skin?

The strip is good for ruling things out quickly; skin shows how the perfume reacts with you, which is what really matters.

How many decants should I try at once?

One per day. That way you perceive the full development without mixing up scents.

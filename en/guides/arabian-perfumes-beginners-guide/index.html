Arabian perfumes: a beginner's guide

Updated October 17, 2026 · By Althamir GT

Arabian perfumes have a long tradition in the Middle East and are recognized by their intense, enveloping and long-lasting scents. Today they are one of the most popular categories among people looking for different fragrances.

Characteristic ingredients

  • Oud (agarwood): a deep, woody, resinous and sometimes smoky scent.
  • Amber: warm, sweet and enveloping.
  • Musk: soft and lasting; it adds body and longevity.
  • Rose and saffron: add a floral and spicy touch.
  • Frankincense, sandalwood and vanilla: resins and creamy woods.

A style of its own

Besides alcohol-based perfumes, the Arabian tradition includes perfumed oils (attar) and bakhoor, aromatic chips that are burned to perfume clothes and spaces. Many houses in the region also make fresh, modern fragrances, so it isn't all intense oud.

How to start trying them

  • Start with families you already like: citrus, sweet gourmand types (date, vanilla) or woody scents.
  • Try oud in small doses: it can be intense, so start with 1 or 2 sprays.
  • Use decants to get to know several houses (Lattafa, Rasasi, Afnan, Al Haramain, Armaf, among others) without buying full bottles.
  • Evaluate longevity and projection on your skin over several hours.

A bit of context

Middle Eastern perfumery has centuries of history tied to the trade in resins, woods and spices. Today, alongside traditional preparations, modern houses coexist that combine that heritage with Western styles.

Common mistakes when starting out

  • Starting with the strongest oud: it can be overwhelming if you don't yet know your taste.
  • Using too many sprays: many perfumes go a long way with very few.
  • Judging a perfume only by the top notes: the base is half the story.

How to read an Arabian perfume's description

Descriptions usually list notes such as oud, amber, rose, saffron, musk or frankincense. If you see oud at the start of the list, it's likely an intense scent; if citrus or fruits appear, it's usually fresher. Also pay attention to words like “intense” or “elixir”: they are commercial names and don't always indicate the concentration.

Most common formats

  • Spray perfume (eau de parfum or extrait): the most practical for daily use.
  • Perfumed oils (attar): applied in small drops on the skin.
  • Bakhoor: aromatic chips to perfume clothes and spaces.

Frequently asked questions

Do they smell like designer perfumes?

Not necessarily. Some are inspired by well-known perfumes, but many have their own identity, with stronger and longer-lasting accords.

Do they work in warm weather?

Yes, as long as you choose fresh versions or use few sprays with the stronger ones.

Why do they usually last so long?

Many have bases rich in amber, musk and woods, and several are formulated with high concentrations. See the guide on concentrations.

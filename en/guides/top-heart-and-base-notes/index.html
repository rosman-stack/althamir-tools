Top, heart and base notes

Updated October 17, 2026 · By Althamir GT

A perfume doesn't smell the same with the first spray as it does a few hours later. The fragrance pyramid describes that evolution in three stages: top, heart and base notes.

The three stages of a perfume

Top notes

It's the first thing you perceive and it evaporates quickly, usually within the first 15 to 30 minutes. They tend to be fresh and light: citrus like lemon or bergamot, herbs and some soft spices.

Heart notes

They appear when the top notes fade and define the perfume's character. They can last a couple of hours. This is where you usually find flowers, fruits and spices such as cinnamon or cardamom.

Base notes

They're the heaviest and the ones that stay on your skin for hours. They include woods (sandalwood, cedar, oud), amber, vanilla, musk and patchouli. They provide longevity and depth. Timings are approximate and vary by perfume and by your skin.

Why it matters when choosing

Many perfumes are bought for the top notes, which are usually the most striking part, and then disappoint when the base shows up. That's why it's best to test them on skin and wait two or three hours before deciding.

How to test a perfume

  1. Apply a spray on your wrist or forearm; don't rely only on a paper strip.
  2. Smell it at 10 minutes, at one hour and at three hours.
  3. Note which notes you like and which you don't, so you can look for similar perfumes.

Examples of families and their notes

  • Citrus: top with lemon, orange or bergamot, heart with herbs and a base of light musk.
  • Floral: a heart of rose, jasmine or orange blossom over a base of musk or sandalwood.
  • Oriental or amber: a spiced heart (cinnamon, cardamom) and a base of amber, vanilla and resins.
  • Woody: aromatic top, a heart of lavender or geranium and a base of cedar, vetiver and patchouli.

If you like it at first but not at the end

It's very common. If you love the top but the base tires you, look for perfumes with a similar top and a lighter base, such as light musks instead of amber or patchouli. If it's the other way around, wait a few minutes before forming an opinion: sometimes the best comes later.

How to read a perfume's description

In stores you'll see the notes organized into top, heart and base. Pay special attention to the base and the fragrance family, because they anticipate how the perfume will feel after the first hour. If notes you already like dominate, such as vanilla or cedar, that's a good sign; if one you can't stand shows up, it's probably not for you.

Remember that notes are a guide, not an exact recipe: two perfumes with the same notes can smell very different depending on the proportions.

Frequently asked questions

Why does a perfume smell different on my skin than on someone else?

Temperature, hydration and the chemistry of each person's skin change how the notes develop.

What is an accord?

It's the combination of several notes that, together, create a new impression, like “amber vanilla” or “woody leather”.

Are the notes in the description all the notes the perfume has?

They're usually the main ones. The full formula has many more components.

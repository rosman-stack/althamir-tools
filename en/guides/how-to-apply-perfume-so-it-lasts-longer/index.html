How to apply perfume so it lasts longer

Updated October 17, 2026 · By Althamir GT

Picking a good perfume is only half the job. How and where you apply it affects how long it lasts and how it's perceived.

Steps for applying it well

  1. Start with clean, dry skin. If you can, apply an unscented moisturizer first: hydrated skin holds perfume better.
  2. Apply to pulse points: neck, wrists, behind the ears and the inner elbows, where skin is warmer.
  3. Spray from about 15 to 20 centimeters (6 to 8 inches) away so the mist spreads evenly.
  4. Let it dry on its own. Rubbing your wrists together doesn't make it last longer.

How many sprays to use

It depends on the concentration and the strength of the scent. As a general guide, 3 to 6 sprays work for most people; if the perfume is very strong or very concentrated, start with 2 or 3. Using more doesn't make it last longer and can tire your nose: you stop noticing it even though others still smell it.

Common mistakes

  • Spraying clothes without testing first: some perfumes can stain delicate fabrics.
  • Spraying straight onto your hair: the alcohol can dry it out.
  • Keeping the bottle in the bathroom or the car, where heat and humidity damage it. See the guide on how to store your perfumes.
  • Combining it with heavily scented lotions or soaps, which compete with the perfume.

Touching up during the day

A 5 or 10 ml decant is handy to carry in your bag and refresh with a couple of sprays if you need to.

Where to apply depending on the moment

  • Office: a couple of sprays on the neck or inner wrist for a close, subtle scent.
  • Night out or event: add a spray on the chest or clothing for more presence.
  • Heat: fewer sprays on more covered areas, so it doesn't feel heavy.

If your skin is dry

On dry skin, perfume tends to evaporate faster. Moisturizing beforehand helps, and perfumes with an amber, vanilla or woody base tend to last longer. You can also try layering: a long-lasting base with a lighter accent.

How to make it last on hot, humid days

  • Choose perfumes with an amber, musk or woody base, which hold up better in the heat.
  • Apply on areas less exposed to direct sweat, like the chest or clothing.
  • Carry a decant to refresh in the afternoon.

A 30-second routine

  1. After showering, moisturize your skin with an unscented lotion.
  2. Apply 2 or 3 sprays on the neck and wrists.
  3. Add one spray on the chest or clothing if you want more presence.
  4. Don't rub: let the perfume settle on its own.

Frequently asked questions

Is it better to apply perfume by day or at night?

There's no rule. Heat makes a scent project more, so on hot days it's better to use fewer sprays.

Can I apply perfume over clothes?

Yes, it usually lasts longer on fabric, but test in a hidden spot first because some perfumes can leave stains.

How long should a perfume last?

It varies a lot: a light citrus may last a couple of hours, while one with an amber or woody base lasts considerably longer. Also see the guide on concentrations.

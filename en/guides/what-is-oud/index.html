What is oud in perfumery?

Updated October 17, 2026 · By Althamir GT

Oud, also called agarwood, is one of the most emblematic ingredients of Arabian perfumery. Its scent is deep, resinous and very distinctive.

Where oud comes from

Oud forms in trees of the Aquilaria genus when they become infected with a mold and the tree responds by producing a dark, aromatic resin. A highly prized oil is obtained from that impregnated wood in the Middle East and Southeast Asia.

Because it's a natural and uncommon process, natural oud can be very expensive. That's why many fragrances use synthetic oud or blends that recreate its character at a more accessible price.

What it smells like

It's woody, resinous and sometimes smoky, with nuances that can range from sweet to leathery or even animalic. There's no single oud smell: it changes depending on the origin and on which notes it's combined with.

What it's usually combined with

  • Rose: a classic pairing that softens the oud with a floral touch.
  • Saffron and spices: add warmth and a spicy nuance.
  • Amber and vanilla: make it sweeter and more enveloping.
  • Sandalwood and musk: make it creamier and softer.

How to start with oud fragrances

  1. Choose soft oud versions first, or ones blended with sweet or floral notes.
  2. Use just 1 or 2 sprays at the beginning: it's an intense, long-lasting scent.
  3. Try it as a decant before buying a full bottle.
  4. Prefer the evening or cool weather; in the heat it comes across stronger.

Oud in layering

Oud works very well as a base. Combined with a citrus or fruity accent it becomes lighter and easier to wear. You can try blends in the Layering Helper.

Natural or synthetic oud

Natural oud is usually more complex and more expensive; synthetic is more accessible and offers a more consistent profile. Neither is better by definition: what matters is that you like the scent and that it fits your budget. If you're still discovering your taste, starting with more affordable versions is a good way to learn what kind of oud you prefer.

Oil or spray

Oud oil is applied in drops, goes a long way and is very concentrated. The spray is more convenient and spreads the scent evenly; to start, it's usually the most practical.

Frequently asked questions

Do all perfumes with oud smell the same?

No. There's fresh, sweet, smoky, floral or creamy oud, depending on the notes it's combined with.

Is oud only for men?

No. It's an ingredient used in masculine, feminine and unisex fragrances.

Does it work in warm climates?

Yes, with few sprays and preferably in the evening. If you wear it by day, choose lighter versions.
